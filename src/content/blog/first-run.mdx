I started running because I was embarrassed not to. A friend mentioned she'd done a 10k and I nodded along like I understood what that meant physically. I didn't. I had not run anywhere on purpose since secondary school.

The first attempt lasted four minutes. Not four minutes of running — four minutes total, including the time I spent standing on the pavement outside my building wondering if anyone could see me from their window.

## the route

I found it by opening Google Maps and looking for something circular near my apartment. Criteria: no hills if I could avoid it, not too much traffic, somewhere I wouldn't run into anyone I knew.

<RunningRoute />

This loop became the only loop for about three weeks. I traced it badly at first — stopping to walk, losing count of where I was, finishing earlier than planned and telling nobody. By week three I was running the whole thing without stopping. That felt genuinely unreasonable, like something that should not have been possible given the previous four minutes.

## what actually changed

I stopped tracking pace. I know people recommend this. I didn't believe it would matter until it mattered immediately. The moment I stopped knowing my pace, running became more like walking — something you just do to get from one place to another, except faster and slightly more painful.

The second thing was a playlist. Not a dedicated running playlist. Just music I liked. Running became the container for twenty-eight uninterrupted minutes of that.

## the honest version

I still don't love running. I think I love having run. The first kilometre is always wrong — some low-grade resistance that doesn't go away, just becomes background noise after a while.

My left shoulder does something strange after fifteen minutes that I've chosen not to investigate.

But I go now, a few times a week, on routes I've expanded since that first loop. I know exactly where the pavement dips near the park entrance. I know which bench is best for stretching and which corner I always speed up at for no reason. I know where the light is good in the morning.

That's probably what people mean when they say they're runners.
